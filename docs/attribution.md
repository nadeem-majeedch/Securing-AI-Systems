# Credits and Attribution

The course is original work that stands on a great deal of other people's work: frameworks, standards, papers, libraries and one dataset. This page says what belongs to whom, so that you can cite it correctly and reuse what you are allowed to reuse.

**Licence status: no licence has been applied yet.** Until the course owner records one, the material is under copyright with all rights reserved. You may read it, study from it and quote it with attribution in academic work; you may not republish or redistribute it. The full reasoning, the recommended licences and the owner's decision record are in `LICENSING.md` in the repository.

## 3. Research

Forty-three core papers, plus standards, frameworks and the primary sources for ten case studies, are cited across the course. The bibliography is [References](references.md), and the annotated entries — with venue, identifier and one line on why it matters — are on the four [research cluster pages](research/index.md).

What the course does with them: it cites them, paraphrases their findings and quotes a sentence where the exact wording matters. It does **not** host PDFs, reproduce figures, or reprint abstracts. Each paper remains under its own licence, which is usually the publisher's or arXiv's, not this course's.

**Citing a paper in your own work.** Use the venue and identifier from the reference entry: author, title, venue, year, DOI or arXiv ID. If the entry in this course helped you find it, cite the paper, not the course page.

> Optical Recognition of Handwritten Digits, E. Alpaydin and C. Kaynak (1998), UCI Machine Learning Repository, <https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/80/optical+recognition+of+handwritten+digits>, licensed CC BY 4.0. Re-exported as an 8×8 image array with targets in this course's laboratory data.

**The course downloads no other dataset.** The laboratory generator is explicit about it: nothing is fetched, everything is computed from a fixed seed.

## 7. How to cite this course

Modules are revised, so cite the module and the date you accessed it:

> Majeed, M. N. *Securing AI Systems*, Module 06: Adversarial Machine Learning. Department of Data Science (PUCIT), University of the Punjab, 2026. Accessed 20 November 2026.

For a whole-course citation, name the course, the author, the department and the year, and add the access date. There is no DOI yet; when one exists it will be recorded here and in `CHANGELOG.md`.

If you adapt the material — a translation, a shortened version for your own class, a localised case study — the current terms require written permission, and once a licence is recorded the adaptation will need to say that it is modified and must not imply that the author or the University endorses it.

## 8. Reporting an attribution problem

If you believe something in this course is attributed incorrectly, used without permission, or missing from the register, report it — through the repository issue tracker, or privately via `SECURITY.md` if it involves a leaked document or personal data. Corrections are made in the next release and recorded in `CHANGELOG.md`.
