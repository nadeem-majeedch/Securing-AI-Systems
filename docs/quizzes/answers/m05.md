## Multiple-choice explanations

**1. M05-Q01** — correct answer **C: Collect only the fields the model uses and justify each one**

Minimisation is a decision made before collection: each field needs a purpose and a justification, because data you never collect cannot leak, be subpoenaed or be mis-joined.

- **A** — Collecting then removing leaves copies in backups, logs and caches.
- **B** — Future use is not a current purpose, and storage does not reduce exposure.
- **D** — Deleting the model does not delete the data or its derivatives.

**2. M05-Q02** — correct answer **B: Re-identification by joining on quasi-identifiers**

Quasi-identifiers combine to single out individuals when joined with another dataset, which is the classic linkage attack and the reason de-identification is not anonymisation.

- **A** — Direct identifiers are only part of identifiability.
- **C** — Accuracy is a utility question, not the privacy risk.
- **D** — Storage cost is irrelevant to the exposure.

**3. M05-Q03** — correct answer **A: Inference of a sensitive value when all five records share it**

k-anonymity hides which record you are, not what the group says: if the equivalence class is homogeneous, membership in the group reveals the sensitive attribute, which l-diversity addresses.

- **B** — k-anonymity does not protect a homogeneous group.
- **C** — Hashes are not reversed, and hashing identifiers is a separate mistake.
- **D** — Table inversion is not the mechanism here.

**4. M05-Q04** — correct answer **D: The privacy loss bound, and therefore the noise scale and utility cost**

Smaller epsilon means a tighter bound on how much any single record can change the output, which requires more noise and costs utility; epsilon is a budget, not a count.

- **A** — Record counts are set by the data holder, not by epsilon.
- **B** — Differential privacy is unrelated to encryption strength.
- **C** — Rate limits are enforcement, not the meaning of epsilon.

**5. M05-Q05** — correct answer **C: Whether the generator memorised and reproduces real records**

A generative model fitted to sensitive data can reproduce it, especially with small samples or overtraining, so synthetic data needs a memorisation test before it is treated as safe.

- **A** — Synthetic data is derived from real data and inherits its risks.
- **B** — Format is an engineering detail.
- **D** — Training time says nothing about disclosure.

**6. M05-Q06** — correct answer **B: The row must be removed from training data and future models, and the current model's influence assessed**

Erasure covers copies and derivatives: delete from the source and training snapshots, retrain or unlearn where required, and assess whether the deployed model still encodes the record before claiming compliance.

- **A** — Source deletion leaves snapshots, caches and trained weights.
- **C** — Refusal is a legal position, not an engineering one, and is often wrong.
- **D** — Retraining is sometimes exactly what is required.

**7. M05-Q07** — correct answer **A: Source, collection method, timestamps, transformations and content hashes**

Provenance is a chain: where each record came from, how it was changed, when, and a digest that proves the bytes you trained on are the bytes recorded.

- **B** — A name and a count cannot distinguish two datasets or detect modification.
- **C** — The model is a consumer of the data, not part of its provenance.
- **D** — Location is metadata, not evidence of origin or integrity.

**8. M05-Q08** — correct answer **D: The posts may contain personal data and injected instructions**

Public text carries both personal data and adversarial content, so scraping imports a privacy obligation and an injection surface that curated corpora do not have.

- **A** — Storage cost is trivial compared with the exposure.
- **B** — Length is a preprocessing issue with known remedies.
- **C** — Language is a filtering problem, not a security property.

**9. M05-Q09** — correct answer **C: The model will rely on a signal that cannot be verified, inviting leakage and drift**

A feature that exists only in training usually encodes information unavailable at decision time, which inflates evaluation and can leak the label, producing a model that fails in production.

- **A** — Capacity is not the issue.
- **B** — Training speed is unrelated to the defect.
- **D** — Encryption is orthogonal to feature availability.

**10. M05-Q10** — correct answer **B: It replaces identifiers with tokens that can be reversed with a key**

Pseudonymisation preserves a mapping held separately, so the data remains personal data for anyone with the key or the ability to link tokens.

- **A** — Reversible linkage is the opposite of anonymisation.
- **C** — Quasi-identifiers are untouched by tokenising names.
- **D** — k-anonymity is a property of groups, not a consequence of pseudonymisation.

## Written model answers

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-s1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-S1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Short answer · 3 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Explain in two sentences why a hash of an email address is still personal data in most threat models.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>A hash is a deterministic function, so anyone who can guess the input can confirm the match by hashing candidate addresses. The set of plausible email addresses is small enough to enumerate, which makes the hash a lookup key rather than an anonymiser.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-s2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-S2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Short answer · 3 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">State the difference between deleting a record and deleting its influence, and why an ML system must consider both.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Deleting a record removes a stored row; deleting its influence removes what a trained model learned from it, which persists in weights and derived artefacts. A system that only deletes rows can still answer questions in a way that reflects the erased record.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-c1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-C1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Conceptual · 5 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Compare k-anonymity, l-diversity and differential privacy on what they protect and what they cost.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>k-anonymity ensures every record is indistinguishable from at least k-1 others on the quasi-identifiers, which is cheap but fails when the group is homogeneous and degrades utility sharply on high-dimensional data. l-diversity adds a requirement that each group contains diverse sensitive values, reducing attribute disclosure at a further utility cost and still vulnerable to skewness attacks. Differential privacy bounds the influence any single record can have on an output, giving a composable guarantee that survives linkage, at the cost of noise calibrated to epsilon and a harder implementation. The honest comparison is that DP offers the strongest statement and the largest engineering burden.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-c2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-C2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Conceptual · 5 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Explain why data retention is a security control and not only a storage-cost decision.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Every retained copy enlarges the set of records that can be breached, subpoenaed, mis-joined or accidentally used in training, and old copies usually sit outside current access controls and monitoring. Retention limits reduce the population at risk and the scope of any future incident, which is why a defensible system can state when each dataset is destroyed and can demonstrate that destruction happened. The control is only real if deletion is scheduled, verified and recorded.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-sc1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-SC1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Scenario-based · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A clinic wants to publish a research dataset of visit records. Propose three releases of increasing utility and state the risk each carries.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Release one: aggregate counts by region and month with small-cell suppression, low utility, low re-identification risk. Release two: a synthetic dataset with a memorisation test and nearest-neighbour disclosure checks, moderate utility, residual risk if the generator overfits. Release three: a differentially private model or query interface with a stated epsilon and a query budget, high utility for modelling, risk concentrated in composition and implementation errors. The recommendation should depend on what the researchers actually need, not on maximal openness.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-sc2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-SC2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Scenario-based · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A hospital discovers that a vendor's de-identification pipeline removed names but left free-text notes unchanged. Describe the exposure and the immediate actions.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Free-text notes routinely contain names, addresses, dates and identifiers, so removing structured name fields leaves the data effectively identifiable. Immediate actions: suspend further disclosure, determine which recipients received which files and when, preserve logs, notify the data-protection officer and assess whether the recipient is a controller or processor. Then rebuild the pipeline with named-entity detection, manual sampling and a re-identification test before any re-release. The root cause is that de-identification was validated against columns rather than content.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-p1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-P1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Practical programming · 8 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Write `k_anonymity(df, quasi_ids, k)` that groups by the quasi-identifier columns, returns the smallest group size and the number of records in groups smaller than k. Test it on a small table where one combination is unique.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>A complete implementation groups by the listed columns, sizes each group, computes the minimum size, and counts rows in groups below k. It should validate that the columns exist and that k ≥ 2. The test builds a table of five rows where one combination occurs once, asserts the minimum is 1 and the count of records in undersized groups is 1. A strong answer also returns the offending combinations so the analyst can generalise or suppress them, rather than only a summary number.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-ct1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-CT1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Critical thinking · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A team argues that because their model never outputs training records verbatim, they do not need to consider dataset privacy. Assess the argument.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>The argument mistakes verbatim recall for disclosure. Privacy attacks exploit statistical differences: membership inference tests whether a record was used, attribute inference estimates a sensitive property, and inversion reconstructs a representative profile. None requires the model to reproduce a row. The correct test is an empirical one, such as a membership-inference advantage measured on members and non-members from the same population, reported with its false-positive rate. Until that measurement exists, the claim is unverified rather than false.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m05-ct2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M05-CT2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Critical thinking · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">You are asked to approve using a public scrape as fine-tuning data. What conditions would make it defensible, and what would rule it out?</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>It is defensible with a lawful basis and a documented purpose, filtering for personal data and prohibited content, provenance records per source, an injection scan, a memorisation and leakage test after fine-tuning, and a removal process for later takedown requests. It is ruled out when the source's terms forbid it, when personal data cannot be filtered at acceptable cost, when the corpus is dominated by content designed to manipulate models, or when no process exists to remove a record after the fact. The decision should name which of these conditions is unmet.</p></div>
