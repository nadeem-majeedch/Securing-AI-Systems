## Multiple-choice explanations

**1. M04-Q01** — correct answer **B: It marks where the set of entities that can influence data changes.**

A boundary is where data or control passes between parties with different privileges, which is exactly where an attacker can insert influence that the receiving side treats as trusted.

- **A** — Format and storage changes happen inside a boundary too and do not imply a trust change.
- **C** — Latency is an operational property, not a security one.
- **D** — Ownership boundaries often coincide with trust boundaries but are not the definition.

**2. M04-Q02** — correct answer **A: Elevation of privilege**

The document is data, but following its instructions gives content the authority of the operator, which is an elevation of privilege through the model's instruction hierarchy.

- **B** — Tampering assumes the data was modified; it may be authored that way deliberately.
- **C** — Spoofing is impersonating an identity, and no identity is being claimed here.
- **D** — Repudiation concerns denying an action, not acquiring authority.

**3. M04-Q03** — correct answer **D: The one with the higher likelihood and lower remediation cost**

When scores tie, the cheaper and more probable fix buys the most reduction per unit of effort; ranking by novelty or seniority is how backlogs become political.

- **A** — Age of a component says nothing about exposure or impact.
- **B** — Presentation quality is not a risk property.
- **C** — Seniority is not evidence.

**4. M04-Q04** — correct answer **C: The risk that remains after controls are applied and verified.**

Residual risk is what is left once controls exist and have been tested; it must be stated explicitly, because a register that shows every row closed is not credible.

- **A** — Removed risk is the reduction, not the remainder.
- **B** — A control failing is a control-effectiveness question, not the definition.
- **D** — Undisclosed risk is a disclosure failure, not a residual quantity.

**5. M04-Q05** — correct answer **B: Identifiability**

Membership inference identifies a person as belonging to a data set, which is identifiability; linkability concerns joining records across sources.

- **A** — Linkability is about combining two data sets, not about membership in one.
- **C** — Non-repudiation is a threat to privacy when actions cannot be denied, and it is not the membership case.
- **D** — Detectability is about discovering whether an item exists, which is adjacent but not the standard mapping here.

**6. M04-Q06** — correct answer **A: Uniform severity removes the information a reader needs to prioritise.**

If everything is high, nothing is; prioritisation is the register's product, and uniform ratings discard the analysis the reader is paying for.

- **B** — Correctness is not the issue; usefulness is.
- **C** — Threat modeling is more important, not less, for sensitive data.
- **D** — A checklist is a different instrument and does not produce prioritisation either.

**7. M04-Q07** — correct answer **D: The likelihood estimate and its justification**

A diagram shows structure; the register adds the adversary reasoning — likelihood, impact and why you believe those numbers.

- **A** — External entities are a standard diagram element.
- **B** — Processes are the core of a data-flow diagram.
- **C** — Data stores are also diagram elements.

**8. M04-Q08** — correct answer **C: Three risks with impact, likelihood, the control and the decision needed**

A decision-maker needs the consequence, the probability, what is already being done and what they must choose; everything else is supporting material.

- **A** — Alphabetical order carries no prioritisation.
- **B** — Category lists describe method, not this system's exposure.
- **D** — Raw tool output shifts the analysis onto the reader.

**9. M04-Q09** — correct answer **B: As residual and unverified, with a test date required**

An untested control is a hypothesis; until effectiveness is measured, the register must not claim reduction, and a test date is the evidence commitment.

- **A** — Deployment is not evidence of effect.
- **C** — Design intent does not establish that the control works.
- **D** — Deferring to an audit leaves the risk unmanaged in the meantime.

**10. M04-Q10** — correct answer **A: It shows alternative paths to the same goal and where to cut them**

A tree makes the attacker's goal explicit and exposes parallel routes, which is what allows a defender to choose a cut that breaks several paths at once.

- **B** — Likelihood estimation is still required to prioritise leaves.
- **C** — No method guarantees completeness.
- **D** — The tree is built on top of the diagram's structure.

## Written model answers

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-s1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-S1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Short answer · 3 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Name two trust boundaries in a RAG assistant that a team is likely to draw incorrectly, and say what goes wrong if they are missed.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>The boundary between the ingested corpus and the retriever is often drawn as internal, although documents arrive from partners and users. The boundary between the model and its tools is often omitted, although a tool call carries the application's authority. Missing the first hides cross-tenant leakage; missing the second hides instruction-driven action.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-s2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-S2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Short answer · 3 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">State the difference between a threat, a vulnerability and a risk in one line each.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>A threat is an adversary's goal and capability; a vulnerability is a weakness that a threat could use; a risk is the likelihood and impact of that use in this deployment, which is what a register records.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-c1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-C1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Conceptual · 5 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Explain why 'we scored every threat with DREAD' is not sufficient as a risk method, and what you would add.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>DREAD produces a number from five judgements that are easy to score inconsistently, and it hides the reasoning behind a single figure, which makes review impossible. It also treats likelihood crudely relative to impact. Add an explicit scenario for each risk, a stated adversary capability assumption, evidence for the likelihood estimate, and a named owner with a decision date. Where data exists, replace judgement with measurement: a tested attack success rate beats a damage rating, because it can be checked.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-c2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-C2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Conceptual · 5 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A team's threat model lists only attacks that their current controls already block. Explain the methodological error and how to correct it.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>The model has been written backwards, from controls to threats, so it can only confirm what is already handled. Threat modeling starts from assets, boundaries and adversary goals, and only then asks which controls exist and whether they are effective. Correct it by enumerating threats without reference to controls, marking each as mitigated, partially mitigated or accepted, and recording the evidence for the claim. The register should contain rows the team cannot close, otherwise the exercise is decoration.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-sc1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-SC1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Scenario-based · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A hospital wants a triage assistant that reads referrals and drafts a summary for a clinician. Produce a five-row threat register with likelihood, impact, control and residual risk.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Rows should cover: a manipulated referral instructing the assistant to omit an allergy (medium likelihood, high impact; control: treat note text as data, output checked against structured fields; residual: novel phrasing); a referral from another trust read by mistake (medium, high; control: tenant scoping before retrieval; residual: misconfigured ACL); the model inventing a finding (high, medium; control: extractive summary with citations and clinician sign-off; residual: citation drift); a stolen clinician session (low, high; control: short-lived tokens and step-up auth; residual: insider misuse); an outage during triage (medium, medium; control: queue and fallback procedure; residual: delayed care). The register must state the assumptions behind each likelihood.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-sc2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-SC2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Scenario-based · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A fintech asks you to threat model a fraud model that is retrained nightly on merchant-reported labels. Name the four highest-value threats and explain why the nightly retraining magnifies one of them.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Threats: label poisoning through a compromised merchant (integrity), adversarial transaction crafting to evade detection (evasion), extraction of the fraud rules through repeated probes (IP), and privacy leakage from a model fitted to individual cardholders (privacy). Nightly retraining magnifies poisoning because a single bad label batch enters the model within hours and then persists across every subsequent model unless training data is versioned and quarantined. The control is provenance plus a rollback path keyed to the training snapshot.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-p1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-P1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Practical programming · 8 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Write `score(register: list[dict]) -&gt; list[dict]` that computes `risk = likelihood * impact` on a 1–5 scale, tags each row with a band, and sorts descending. Assert that a row missing a justification is rejected rather than scored.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>A complete answer validates that each row has integer likelihood and impact in 1–5 and a non-empty `justification`; rows failing validation raise or are returned as errors rather than being silently scored. Risk is the product, banded by explicit thresholds such as ≥15 high, ≥8 medium, else low. Sorting is descending by risk and then by impact, which keeps ties stable and explainable. The test supplies one valid high row and one row with no justification and asserts the second is reported, not scored.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-ct1-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-CT1</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Critical thinking · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">Your threat model says prompt injection is 'medium' because the assistant has no tools. Six months later the product adds an email tool. What does this reveal about the method, and how should the register be maintained?</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>It reveals that the rating depended on an architectural fact that was never recorded as an assumption, so the risk silently became stale when the fact changed. A register should attach each rating to its preconditions, and the change process should trigger a review when a precondition is broken, in this case any new capability. Injection without tools is an output-quality problem; with a send capability it becomes data exfiltration, so both likelihood and impact change. Maintenance is a change-triggered review, not an annual document refresh.</p></div>

<div class="sas-answer" id="m04-ct2-answer"><p class="sas-item__head"><strong>M04-CT2</strong> <span class="sas-meta">Critical thinking · 6 marks</span></p><p class="sas-answer__q">A regulator asks for evidence that residual risk is 'acceptable'. Argue that acceptability is a decision rather than a measurement, and say what evidence can support it.</p><p class="sas-answer__label">Model answer</p><p>Acceptability involves a value judgement about who bears the harm and whether the benefit justifies it, which no measurement can settle: the same residual risk can be acceptable for a convenience feature and unacceptable for clinical triage. Evidence can still inform the decision: quantified residual risk with its method, a comparison to the previous release or to a baseline, controls verified by test, a stated monitoring and rollback plan, and the identity of the person accountable. The deliverable is a decision record that cites that evidence, not a number labelled acceptable.</p></div>
