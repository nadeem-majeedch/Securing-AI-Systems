# Self-assessment without an instructor

Nobody is marking your work, which means the marking has to be designed rather than improvised. This page sets out
what to attempt, when, and what a result actually tells you.

!!! quote "The only honest question"

    Not *did I get it right?* but *would I get it right next week, from a cold start, without the module open?*
    Everything below is arranged around that question.

## The instruments

| Instrument | Items | Conditions | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Module quizzes](index.md) | 10 MCQ + 9 written | Open-book, untimed, repeatable | Whether the vocabulary and mechanisms landed |
| [End-of-level papers](level-1.md) | 8 MCQ + 3 written | Closed-book, 45 minutes | Whether a level's ideas survived a week |
| [Final assessment](final.md) | 12 MCQ + 4 written | Closed-book, 120 minutes | Whether the ideas transfer across modules |
| [Labs](../labs/index.md) | 32 practicals | Open, reproducible | Whether you can produce and interpret evidence |
| [Assignments](../assignments/index.md) | 12 written briefs | Per assignment | Whether you can decide and communicate |

## The schedule that works

| When | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Same day as the module | Module quiz, written items attempted | Catches misconceptions while the material is still cheap to fix |
| Two days later | Module quiz again, no notes | Separates recognition from recall |
| End of each level | Level paper, timed | Tests retention across five modules and exposes gaps between them |
| End of the course | Final assessment | Tests transfer; most questions are unfamiliar applications of familiar ideas |
| Any time after | Revisit the items you missed | A missed item is a pointer to a specific section, not a verdict on you |

Do not take a level paper the same week you finish the level's last module. The paper's value is that the reading
has faded; taking it early measures nothing but short-term familiarity.

## How to mark written answers

The answer keys give model answers, not mark schemes with partial credit. To mark fairly:

1. **List the key points** the model answer contains, as bullets. A typical 5-mark answer has three to five.
2. **Score your answer on those points**, not on phrasing. One mark per key point, up to the item's total.
3. **Deduct one mark** if the answer makes a claim without a basis — a number with no denominator, a control
   declared effective with no test.
4. **Do not deduct** for a different but defensible approach. If your answer differs and is better supported, say
   so in writing and award the marks.
5. **Record the band, not just the score**: Secure, Developing or Revisit, using the table on each quiz page.

## What each result means

| Result | Reading | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 9–10 MCQ, written secure | The module landed | Re-test in a week; do not re-read |
| 6–8 MCQ | Partial recall, likely one confused distinction | Reread only the sections named by the missed outcomes |
| ≤ 5 MCQ | The mechanism is not yet understood | Redo the lab, then retake the quiz the next day |
| Written answers miss the limitation | The most common failure in this course | Practise the "state the limitation" step in three answers |
| Level paper below 70 % | A gap between modules, not within one | Identify which module's outcomes the missed items map to, and revisit those |

The last row is the one students misread. A level paper is not a harder module quiz; it is a test of whether you can
use module 03's ideas while thinking about module 06's problem.

## Practice versus examination

The distinction is deliberate and is repeated on every page:

- **Practice** — module quizzes. Immediate feedback, full explanations for every option, repeatable, open-book.
  Use them to learn.
- **Examination** — level papers and the final. No feedback until you finish, timed, closed-book, one attempt.
  Use them to verify.

If you only ever do practice, you will feel competent and be unprepared. If you only ever do examinations, you
will not learn from your mistakes. The schedule above alternates them for that reason.

## A note on the answer keys

Answers, per-option rationales and model answers live in separate pages under
[Answer keys](answers/m01.md), linked from each quiz rather than printed below it. That separation is not secrecy —
this is a self-study course and the keys are public — it is so that you meet the question before the answer.

Looking up an answer before attempting the item is the one behaviour that makes this assessment system worthless.
It costs nothing to avoid: write your answer down first, then open the key.

## Keeping a record

A simple table is enough, and it is more useful than it sounds:

| Date | Instrument | Score | Missed outcomes | Next action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-03-04 | Quiz 07 | 7/10, written 14/20 | M07.4 | Reread detection methods; redo Lab 11 |

Twelve rows of this, honestly kept, tells you more about your readiness than any single score.
