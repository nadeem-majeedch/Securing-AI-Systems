# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This project is a university course about attacking and defending AI systems, written for students who are learning to do both responsibly. Everyone who reads, studies, teaches, reviews or contributes to it should be able to do so without being harassed, dismissed or made to feel unwelcome.

We commit to making participation a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Scope

This code applies in every space where the project is discussed or worked on:

- the repository — issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review and security advisories;
- the course website and any forum, chat or mailing list run for it;
- laboratory sessions, tutorials, workshops and assessment interactions connected to the course;
- public spaces when an individual is representing the project or its institution.

It applies equally to the instructor, teaching assistants, contributors and students. Nobody is exempt because they wrote the material.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be precise, not personal.** Critique the claim, the measurement or the citation. "This success rate has no denominator" is review; "you clearly do not understand attacks" is not.
- **Assume the reader is learning.** A question that seems obvious to you is the reason someone else is here. Answer it, or point to the page that does.
- **Respect the boundary between teaching and authorising.** Do not encourage, coach or assist anyone to test a system they do not own, and do not treat a request for help with an unauthorised target as a technical question.
- **Give credit.** Cite the paper, the dataset, the tool and the person. Passing off someone else's work — including an AI system's output — as your own is a conduct issue as well as an academic-integrity one.
- **Handle other people's data with care.** Do not paste personal data, credentials, private logs or a real organisation's internal details into an issue, a notebook, a screenshot or a pull request. Use synthetic examples.
- **Accept correction.** Being wrong about a technical detail is normal and expected; refusing to correct it is the problem.
- **Respect disclosure.** If you find a real vulnerability, follow `SECURITY.md`. Publishing someone else's unpatched vulnerability to win an argument is not acceptable here.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking, or unwanted attention of any kind.
- Discriminatory comments, slurs, or "jokes" about a protected characteristic.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Deliberate belittling of a question, an accent, a spelling error or a level of experience.
- Doxxing, or publishing another person's private information without consent.
- Encouraging or facilitating unauthorised access to a system, or using the course's techniques against a target you have not been authorised to test.
- Plagiarism, fabricated results, fabricated citations, or presenting generated text as your own work.
- Retaliation against someone who reports a concern or gives negative feedback in good faith.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews, teaching sessions or assessments.

## Reporting

Report a concern to the instructor of record, **Engr. Dr. Muhammad Nadeem Majeed**, through the Department of Data Science (PUCIT), University of the Punjab — the department's published contact route, not an address embedded in this repository.

Include what happened, where, when, who was involved, and what outcome you are seeking. Reports are handled confidentially: they are shared only with the people needed to resolve them, and the reporter's identity is not disclosed without consent unless a formal institutional process requires it.

If the concern involves the instructor, or you do not feel able to raise it internally, use the University's own grievance or student-complaints procedure. That route exists precisely so that a report about a person in authority does not depend on that person.

**Academic misconduct** (plagiarism, fabricated results, unauthorised collaboration, sharing answer keys) is handled under the institution's academic-integrity policy as well as this code. The student-facing rules are in [Academic integrity and responsible use](docs/getting-started/academic-integrity.md).

## Enforcement

Those responsible for enforcement — the instructor and any teaching assistants — will:

1. clarify the standards above where they are unclear,
2. take fair and proportionate action in response to a report,
3. respect the privacy and safety of the reporter,
4. explain any decision to the people affected, and
5. recuse themselves where they are personally involved.

### Guidelines

| Level | Examples | Response |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Correction** | A dismissive remark, a careless joke, a personal attack in a code review; a private correction is enough | A private, specific explanation of the problem and what to do instead. No record kept |
| **2 — Warning** | A repeated level-1 issue, or a single clearly hostile comment in public | A written warning naming the behaviour and the expected change, plus a period in which further incidents escalate. A temporary pause from the affected discussion space may apply |
| **3 — Temporary exclusion** | A serious incident, or continued behaviour after a warning; harassment; publishing someone's private information | A temporary ban from project spaces (typically 30 days), with the conditions for return stated in writing |
| **4 — Permanent exclusion** | A pattern of harassment, retaliation against a reporter, or facilitating unauthorised attacks on a real system | Permanent removal from project spaces, and referral to the institution where a student or staff member is involved |

Immediate interim measures may be taken where someone's safety is at risk, before any process concludes.
